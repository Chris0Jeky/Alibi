Versioned solution: shared-signal-1 · Original fictional case

The joint deduction

B1 establishes exactly one open shutter. A1 therefore establishes a pulse. A2 says exactly one signal is on when there is a pulse. B2 rules out Yard, so Quay is on. Cite A1, A2, B1 and B2.

A0 was never installed and is explicitly superseded by revision B. It cannot replace a current premise. None of the four current cards identifies which shutter is upper: either upper-open/lower-closed or upper-closed/lower-open fits the same pulse, Quay-on and Yard-off state.

Formal boundary

Five binary variables give 32 worlds. Archive A1+A2 leaves 6; field B1+B2 leaves 8. Both together leave 2. Removing any one current premise leaves 4 worlds, including Quay-on and Quay-off possibilities. A contradictory Yard-on observation added to all four leaves zero worlds; zero worlds cannot certify a conclusion.

The checker reasons only from selected current cards. An unsupported guess cannot become an extra premise. No claim about a motive, unobserved fault or event outside the inspection bell follows from this model.

Run a comparison only with consent

Give a pair the separate Archive and Field player sheets, or give a solo reader the Combined sheet. They contain the same source text and puzzle. Keep this solution separate. Use different participants for the conditions, or record counterbalancing and carry-over; repeating the same solved case is not a fresh baseline.

Invite participants to explain privately what each perspective contributed, where they were confused, whether one person did all the reasoning, and any accessibility difficulty. Record contrary outcomes as well as positive ones, only with explicit consent. This prototype records and sends nothing.

If the split creates only transcription or waiting, stop before transport or integration. Formal necessity does not establish balanced roles, useful discussion, learning, enjoyment or demand. Issue #451 stays open for this human evidence and a separate promotion decision.
