The lantern reports

At the small harbour of Merewick, five reports reached the night clerk after the bell. Each described the same instant. Before writing a confident account, the clerk needs to find which reports cannot fit together — and what a quieter set of evidence still leaves open.

A standalone reasoning experiment. No timer or sound. Rehearsal stays in memory and clears on reload.

All reports concern the same bell, with no clock uncertainty. Each of the East, West and Stair lanterns was either lit or dark. “Not both lit” allows either one to be lit, or both to be dark. These are the only rules.

Find the smallest conflict

Select the fewest reports that cannot all be true together. There may be more than one equally small answer. Selecting every report is not enough.

    Two smallest conflicts

    R1 and R2 light East and West, but R3 forbids both. R1 and R4 light East and Stair, but R5 forbids both. Either triple works. Every group of one or two reports has at least one possible state, so three really is the smallest size.

    A possible state that defeats “must”

    East dark, West lit, Stair dark. R2 is true because West is lit. R3 and R5 are true because East is dark. Stair is dark, so the claim is false. Stair lit would also fit the same premises: they leave its state open.

    If no state fitted the premises, that would be inconsistent evidence, not proof of the claim and not a counterexample.
