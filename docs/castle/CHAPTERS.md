# Wrenmere story production

Spoilers. This is a proposed continuation of the supplied Wrenmere package, not a claim that later chapters are playable. Chapter I's current content remains authoritative for its published records. Compare the original full story bible before expanding these notes.

## People

Mara Vale curated the house's collection and the planned exhibition, Questions We Share. Show her correspondence, practical work and interest in other people's methods. Her life should occupy more space than the description of her death.

Rowan Finch maintained the instruments and clocks. His notes are exact when he has measurements; they should identify their assumptions when he does not. The initial accusation against him rests on a station ticket read without the clock correction.

Iona Bell, the housekeeper, knows how people moved through the working parts of the estate. She distinguishes what she saw from what she heard. Ada Vale, Mara's sister, wants the public account corrected without turning Mara into another tidy commemorative anecdote.

Trustee Edmund Wren deferred maintenance and later supported an incomplete public account. Investigate the decisions and their documentation. Do not simplify the story into a premeditated murder for which every puzzle conveniently supplies a clue.

The present-day keeper has practical reasons for asking the visitor to sort the papers. Use that relationship for small observations and dry humour; avoid making the keeper a dispenser of philosophical slogans.

## Chapter I: The Seventeenth Minute

Implemented in the PR stack. The ticket reads 21:17; the station clock is documented as seventeen minutes fast. A seven-minute route allows departure at 21:00 and arrival at 21:07 before the calibrated 21:10 tower bell. These records establish feasibility. They do not establish Finch's actual presence.

The study opens onto a service stair. An unsigned margin says the writer saw Mara take the courtyard stair towards the river. It does not identify her purpose, confirm arrival, or prove who wrote it. Keep those limits in later retellings.

## Chapter IV: The crossing

#54. Corroborate Finch's movements and reconstruct the evacuation using rescue lists, paths and capacity constraints. The original outline proposes Finch at the tower by 21:08, work on the mechanism by 21:14, Mara taking the service route at 21:12, a village warning at 21:21 and a crossing failure around 21:24.

Do not expose these later timestamps in Chapter I or treat planned details as already proven. Require records with clear provenance and allow approximate times where the fictional evidence warrants them. Mara dies during the flood; no narration can legitimately quote her unrecorded final thoughts. Do not claim a different decision would certainly have saved her.

## Chapter V: The public account

#54. Compare the printed report, missing architectural information, rescue evidence and maintenance history. The proposed account omits the service stair and misuses the fast-clock ticket. Its claim about saving the collection needs to be tested against what the player has actually recovered.

Conclude with a supported correction and choices about preserving or presenting the history. Avoid a points-based judgement of mourning, forgiveness or memorial taste. The visitor can finish with some questions unanswered where the evidence genuinely stops.
