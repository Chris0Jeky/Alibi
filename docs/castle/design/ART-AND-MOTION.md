# Art and motion package

## Supplied now

The estate and room scenes are original procedural SVG compositions authored for this proposal. They use the palette and system-font direction in DESIGN.md. The styles are intentionally illustrated rather than photorealistic. There are no copied Blue Prince scenes, museum photographs, stock dependencies or newly modelled Blender pets.

The portable build includes the scene code and an 18-second silent prologue. The movie is 1280x720 H.264, with visible written captions, four original scenes, small camera movement and fades. A poster, WebVTT captions and plain-text transcript are supplied. It is a mood film, not recorded gameplay and not a claim about shipped Alibi features.

The rendered film was made locally with SVG rasterisation, Pillow and FFmpeg. It was not rendered with HyperFrames. `tools/render-film.py` is the reproducible source. `SERIF_FONT` and `SANS_FONT` can point to installed fonts on another system; font files are not included in the package.

Original SVG exports for the implemented rooms and both estate eras are in media/rooms. These can be used as reference compositions or replaced by richer artwork while preserving the accessible controls. Runtime art is generated by src/art.js; exported vectors are separate editable assets.

## Production art direction

Keep a coherent material vocabulary: wet slate, aged paper, worn oak, cold glass, oxidised copper and patient vegetation. The palette should shift by wing without making every room a different product. Architectural proportions, window shapes and recurring maker marks can connect scenes across decades.

A room needs a strong silhouette before detail. The Observatory has its dome and telescope. The Library has long shelf rhythm and a pool of desk light. The Orangery has a glass grid and leaf silhouettes. The working rooms use repeatable mechanical shapes. Below ground, a shared waterline is more useful than generic darkness.

For future asset generation, supply a common exterior reference, room layout, camera framing, material sheet and forbidden elements. Generate an empty readable scene first, then add important clues as deterministic UI or reviewed overlays. Never rely on generated text, numbers, labels or maps being correct. Those belong to source-authored layers that can be tested and translated.

## Motion hierarchy

The first invitation may be cinematic. Routine navigation should be short. Puzzle solving should be stable. A clue reveal can move one small object. The Quiet Wing can support gentle optional ambient loops, but they must pause when hidden, respect reduced motion and stop when the activity is disposed.

Do not use camera movement to conceal slow loading. A static poster and clear progress/cancel state are better. Do not fade readable clue text while the player is selecting it. A browser's back action should not trigger another introduction.

Production videos remain optional lazy assets. The standalone file embeds the film only for portability. The LITE build omits it and provides a text alternative. Do not ship the embedded base64 film inside the normal app's initial JavaScript.

## HyperFrames handoff

HyperFrames is suitable for a later production composition because the artwork, text layers and timing can remain editable HTML. Use the installed HyperFrames skill in Codex before authoring that version. Carry over DESIGN.md, the four SVG scenes, the poster framing and prologue transcript. Keep the timeline deterministic, register a paused composition timeline, and avoid infinite loops or async timeline construction. Render and inspect the actual output before replacing this film.

This is a handoff direction, not an installed or executed HyperFrames composition. No external generation job or paid service was submitted in this pass.
