# Writing at Wrenmere

Use the room and the speaker. The keeper can be dry, Finch exact, Iona practical, Ada impatient with the commemorative plaque. Let them disagree in the choice of details as well as in their opinions. Avoid making everyone speak in polished maxims.

A clue states what can be observed, including units and uncertainty. Feedback names the move or inference to revisit. Completion text changes something in the room or adds a record. Do not congratulate the player at length or explain the product's philosophy after every solve.

Vary sentence length. Leave some thoughts plain. Give a room one or two physical details worth remembering. Cut ornamental oppositions, repeated three-part phrases, rhetorical questions used as slogans, and the repeated “not X, but Y” turn. A contrast can stay when it is the actual subject of a deduction. Do not weaken “possible versus proved” merely to avoid a phrasing rule.

Examples:

| Avoid | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Every shelf has an order. Every account has a gap. | A maintenance slip has been pushed behind the wrong book. |
| A method, not a shortcut. | Finch used the three pegs to teach apprentices. The smallest disk has been mended twice. |
| The evidence does not yet support this conclusion. | The ticket gives a departure time. Which record places him at the tower? |
| Unlock an unforgettable journey of discovery. | The cupboard opens onto a narrow stair. |

Keep interface labels literal: Undo, Check, Open notebook, Export castle save. Room prose can carry texture; a button should tell the player what happens.

Historical exhibits keep their source, date qualifications and model assumptions. Wrenmere's documents are fiction. Mark an unsigned account as unverified until another record corroborates it. Preserve uncertainty through the ending. Hints may explain a rule or suggest a representation; worked answers are a separate, labelled action.

Review copy by reading a sequence of screens aloud. A mechanical phrase scan may flag repetition, but it cannot approve a voice. Required clues must remain available as text, with equivalent keyboard controls, and without sound or animation.
