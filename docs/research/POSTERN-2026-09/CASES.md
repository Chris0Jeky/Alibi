# Original case treatments

All six are newly proposed fiction. They are not additions to Wrenmere's published canon, release promises or tested difficulty estimates. Each requires authored source records, a bounded semantic model where suitable, editorial review and player observation before promotion. The first workbench specimen should be small enough to reject or substantially revise cheaply.

## 1. The Reading Room Blackout: the workbench specimen

A folio is missing from an exhibition stand. A last-seen note, inspection sheet, maintenance record and visitor recollections establish different things. The player narrows the interval, discovers why a silent door alarm proves little, and writes a conclusion that does not falsely identify the person responsible.

Loop: inspect sources, classify statements, choose sufficient citations, test an alternative, then move to the next question. Use three short steps with supported/contradicted/not-established verdicts and an explicitly requested hint ladder. The ending establishes what can be corrected in the catalogue and what still needs investigation, rather than manufacturing a confession.

Semantic model: enumerate the stated finite time slots and possible people under the fictional record constraints. A proposition is supported if true in every admissible model, contradicted if false in every model, otherwise not established. This proves only those stated assumptions; it is not a general natural-language theorem prover. Expose the model's simplifications in the author notes.

Boundary: no real crime, no Wrenmere character rewrite, no player storage. The HTML is an experimental memory-only authoring preview, not a new official live game. Measure whether citation selection is satisfying reasoning or unnecessary bookkeeping.

## 2. The Wrong Tide Table

The quay display seems to prove a courier arrived after the tide cut off a causeway. Two tables use different harbour reference points, and a repair notice limits the route independently of the tide. Reconcile units, place and validity dates before interpreting the itinerary.

Mechanics: table selection, interval overlap, map alternatives. Sources: two explicit fictional tables, their legends, a dated repair notice and one limited witness statement. The reveal separates a possible crossing from a documented one. Do not simulate real tides or imply navigation advice. This can later inform #77 without overwriting its chronology.

## 3. The Herbarium's Second Label

A specimen has two accession labels with different names. A gardener's notebook and catalogue revision reveal a change in classification, not necessarily a forged object. The player reconstructs its object biography and chooses an honest museum caption.

Mechanics: dated document ordering, stable object identity, source comparison and uncertain attribution. Warm glasshouse setting, paper/wood feedback, complete text descriptions of visual features. Any real botanical or historical interpretation needs primary-source review; use explicitly fictional cultivars for an early prototype. Connect to #50, not a second museum registry.

## 4. The Translator's Supper

Two visiting workshops use different symbols for ownership, requests and shared tools. A kitchen misunderstanding is resolved by learning a small original grammar from exchanges rather than substituting letters. The final request uses a combination never shown verbatim.

Mechanics: contextual observation, compositional language, revisable lexicon, optional complementary dossiers. There is no stealth tax or timer. Symbol shapes need accessible names and a nonvisual structural description. Test language/localisation bias before expanding; no borrowed script or culture from S2.

## 5. The Room on Neither Plan

A service route connects familiar places differently across two surveys. Stable walls, door swing and accession dates allow the drawings to be aligned. A source was omitted later, but the author's motive remains unknown.

Mechanics: bounded overlay alignment with named transforms and adjacency constraints, then witness terminology. This is a treatment supporting existing #53, not a competing Chapter II rewrite. The missing Iona reference and independent garden record already identified in CONTINUATION.md must be authored before granting attribution or route-direction claims.

## 6. The Shared Signal

An archivist holds maintenance instructions; a fieldworker sees the installed shutters. Neither dossier alone determines which signal reached the quay. Both players reconstruct intended, installed and observed networks, then decide what the documents fail to settle.

Mechanics: asymmetric information, small network reducer, joint source-linked conclusion. Begin with printable/offline sheets. Offer a solo combined dossier and untimed mode. A later Wrenmere adaptation must respect #76 and the source chronology. Networking is rejected unless the paper trial shows conversation adds useful deduction.

## Shared editorial requirements

Every treatment needs a source inventory, dependency graph, chronology, permitted conclusions, plausible wrong conclusions, remaining uncertainty, hint ladder, story-off copy, rights ledger and explicit ending condition. A sensory flourish cannot be the only clue. A red herring must be distinguishable through evidence, not arbitrary author preference. A reveal should explain why an alternative fails without ridiculing the player.
