# Product thesis and current-product audit

## The proposed promise

**Enter a place worth returning to. Notice something. Work out what it means. Leave with a discovery you can explain.**

The intended product is not an endless menu, an AI improvisation demo or a single enormous adventure. It is a coherent house with three complementary experiences: quick independent puzzles, authored evidence-led cases, and longer room-based mysteries. Workshop and optional social sessions extend those experiences rather than compete for the home screen.

This is a design thesis, not an empirically proven positioning statement. Sources S1–S5 in [SOURCES](SOURCES.md) motivate exploration, interpretable systems and authoring tools; they do not prove this combination will succeed.

## Audience contracts

A five-minute visitor can start an appropriate puzzle without reading lore. A mystery player can investigate an original question with fair evidence and meaningful uncertainty. A returning visitor can recover their place without replaying exposition. A creator can understand why a case is invalid before exporting it. A player who does not hear, drag, distinguish colours or enjoy timers receives the same relevant information.

Difficulty is not hostility. Keep advanced problems, but remove ambiguous controls, avoidable repetition and hidden prerequisites. Hints teach a method before revealing an answer. Revising a theory should feel like progress rather than losing a life.

## What Alibi already offers

Observed at the pinned baseline: a 510-puzzle, 13-family cabinet; five casebooks; six Games Room activities; 95 curated challenges; local Workshop packs; garden/realm/companions; ten castle questions, collected evidence and a hypothesis notebook. There is already a substantial local-first delivery, validation, replay and recovery stack. These are strengths to extend, not justification for another root framework or save database. See the evidence paths in [SOURCES](SOURCES.md).

The live backlog already owns Wrenmere art, objects, sensory feedback, evidence-board expansion, later chapters, museum work and catalogue familiarity. Some broad issue bodies are older than the implementation. Start each task by inspecting code and linked receipts, not assuming an open issue means nothing has been built.

## Main constraints that change the strategy

**Delivery headroom:** application and castle bundles are close to existing ceilings. New cases must be authored separately before deciding whether to preload them. Do not disguise optional bytes or raise limits just to pass. The collection install path belongs to #433, with #389's loading/identity gaps retained.

**Canonical story:** Chapter I establishes a feasible route, not Finch's actual presence. Later scenes cannot turn uncertainty into fact through decorative prose. Character identities, era, river geography and source provenance need one reviewed ledger.

**Data ownership:** Cabinet, Club, Quiet Wing, challenge and castle stores have existing authorities. A unified journal is a read surface over them, not a new competing database. Unknown or newer saves remain protected. The workbench prototype is intentionally memory-only and cannot modify live saves.

**Quality evidence:** mathematical uniqueness, an editorial reasoning route, automated controls, real-device accessibility and player enjoyment are different assertions. Do not let one green test stand in for all five. The owner's data-first calibration choice remains authoritative.

## Differentiation to test

1. **Source literacy as play.** Distinguish record, observation, model and hypothesis through enjoyable deductions. Ask what a clue permits, not merely which suspect to select.
2. **Methods that travel.** A small museum interaction teaches a technique reused in a different-looking room. Transfer must be genuine, not a lore quiz.
3. **A calm, persistent place.** Rooms, materials and character voices create continuity without daily punishment or forced waiting.
4. **Fair authoring at scale.** Creators see dependency, visibility, citation and revision problems early. A small library of excellent cases beats generated filler.
5. **Conversation rather than races.** Optional complementary dossiers make players explain observations. Solo equivalents remain available where reasonable.

## Editorial and business boundaries

Prefer original mysteries beyond murder: miscatalogued objects, lost messages, restoration errors, disputed routes, mechanical failures and competing reconstructions. Stakes can be intimate without being trivial. Do not moral-score grief, punish a player's preferred exhibition treatment or turn incomplete testimony into omniscient blame.

No monetisation change is implemented or assumed here. A later business study may compare a generous free cabinet plus paid authored anthologies, optional supporter cosmetics or a one-off collection. Do not sell hints, remove earned tools, lock essential accessibility or force a subscription before willingness-to-pay research and rights review. These are alternatives for a later decision, not recommendations to launch billing now.

## What not to copy

Do not import mandatory timers from a party game, procedural room resets into Wrenmere's geography, proprietary glyphs/art, an adversarial streak economy, a heavy GPU baseline, or free-form model output as canonical evidence. Do not equate higher puzzle counts with richer mystery. A feature earns its place when it improves deduction, orientation, authorship, atmosphere or meaningful social exchange.
