# Twelve original escape-room treatments

Commissioning portfolio under #462. These are newly proposed fiction, not copied commercial rooms, canonical Wrenmere chapters, calibrated difficulty ratings or twelve shipped levels. Rooms 1-2 have concrete first-specimen puzzle facts below. Rooms 3-12 require complete source packets and semantic models before implementation. All have solo/untimed and complete text-control paths.

## 1. Tidekeeper's Workshop

**Role/objective:** reopen a workshop's small service gate after a routine mechanical reset. The setting is a fictional estuary institution; its gauge is not navigation advice. A dispatch desk, instrument bench, cabinet and lifting gear form a compact scene.

**Two leads:** the cabinet accepts rack marks in launch order. Marks are Snipe 9, Heron 4, Tern 7, Plover 2. Snipe immediately precedes Heron; Tern is neither first nor last; Plover follows Tern. Only Snipe-Heron-Tern-Plover satisfies all three clues, yielding 9472. A separate instrument record says the counter has a fixed additive offset: true levels 2 and 8 produced readings 5 and 11. Its current reading 8 therefore means true level 5.

**Recombination:** open the cabinet, move its one handle to the winch, calibrate the gauge, select the middle notch from 2/5/8 and route the clutch to Lift rather than Return. The gate can always be attempted, but only the complete setup engages it. Moving the handle or changing settings is reversible; wrong attempts lose nothing.

**Aha/risk:** a displayed reading is not the physical quantity, and both branches matter. Do not hide the attempt button when the setting is wrong or expose the correct code in an action label. This is a compact teaching specimen, not an advertised expert room. Its puzzle facts require independent enumeration and arithmetic tests.

## 2. Printmaker's Cabinet

**Role/objective:** produce a valid dispatch proof and open the collection hatch. A job rack, plate cabinet, fictional rehearsal press, routing plan and hatch give each operation a purpose.

**Discovery:** four lettered jobs A-D must be stored in order. B immediately precedes A; D lies after A and before C. The unique order is BADC. Opening the cabinet reveals one plate, which can be carried, fitted and removed without duplication.

**Transformation:** the plate reads KCOD. The rehearsal press offers Face (same order) and Mirror (reverse order); its calibration sample must read DOCK. Match the plate's triangle registration mark to the center triangle, apply ink and use Mirror. A successful impression creates a new, persistent proof rather than another identical lock screen.

**Final deduction:** the proof lists Quay stamp 6, Archive stamp 2, Loft stamp 9. The plan gives distances 4, 2 and 3 respectively. The hatch requires stamps in ascending distance, so the derived sequence is 296. The proof remains valid if the press is adjusted afterward; it records the earlier successful operation.

**Risk:** clearly label the transfer rules as this fictional device's rules. Do not require real printmaking knowledge or make a mirrored visual clue inaccessible in text. The final sequence must be deduced from two records, not presented already in the required order.

## 3. Moonseed Conservatory

**Objective:** align the nursery's shutters to expose a seed drawer without warming the wrong beds. The key operation is combining overlapping patterns, not identifying a color name.

**Structure:** a lens/tool path reveals which beds each shutter affects; a gardener's log establishes the target pattern. For a small proving fixture, masks A={1,2,4}, B={2,3}, C={4,5} toggle numbered beds; target {1,3,5} is reached by combining all three masks. Explain the toggle rule in-room. Later authored variants can add interacting constraints without merely increasing the board size.

**Transformation:** correctly lit beds expose accession marks on a newly reachable tray. **Acceptance:** independently test the mask model and all valid configurations, provide a numbered text diagram, reuse a suitable existing binary-toggle reducer only after equivalence checks, and reject the room if it feels like a detached Lights Out board with a plant skin. #472.

## 4. Herbarium Lift

**Objective:** lower a specimen drawer using two balanced brackets while retaining the original accession labels.

**Sources/mechanics:** three counterweights, Fern, Ash and Ivy, have unreliable label positions but trusted comparison records: Fern+Ash=5, Ash+Ivy=8, Fern+Ivy=7. Their masses are 2, 3 and 5. The lift requires two occupied brackets totaling five, so Fern and Ash are the valid pair; Ivy alone has the same total but leaves a bracket unoccupied. State the bracket rule visibly, not as a surprise rejection.

**Parallel lead:** accession chronology identifies which drawer can safely move. **Transformation:** the lift makes an upper label readable and connects the mechanism to the object biography. **Risk:** don't confuse a changed classification with a forged specimen. Numeric facts, ordering and alternate valid arrangements need separate proofs. #472; coordinate museum #50.

## 5. Rain Collector

**Objective:** distribute a fixed supply among demonstration basins to release a float-operated cabinet. All quantities and transfer rules are fictional and discrete.

**Structure:** identify basin capacities from marked rods, then plan transfers under conservation. A movable connector determines which pair can exchange water; the connector is reused, never consumed. A second path identifies which float reading controls the cabinet. The room should reward planning and recognizing an invariant, not fast repeated clicks.

**Transformation:** the final water distribution raises an object into view. **Acceptance:** select actual capacities/target only after exhaustive reachability proves both a solution and recovery from every legal transfer state. Do not publish an attractive but unreachable target. Provide fill-level text, undo and a reset that restores the complete resource total. This treatment intentionally does not invent a solved numerical instance before that analysis. #472.

## 6. Fogbound Exchange

**Objective:** route a delivery message to the correct quay using two complementary signal records.

**Structure:** one branch recovers the codebook edition; another restores a shutter bank and reveals the actual received signal. A protocol sheet distinguishes intended, sent and observed states. The final route depends on both edition and observation, not a guess about the sender's motives.

**Transformation:** operating the shutters changes which receiving panel can be inspected. **Co-op option:** operator and archivist views can divide these sources, with a solo combined view. **Acceptance:** codebook ambiguity and parity errors must be explicit; provide identical symbolic text and visual information. No real network protocol, radio hardware or mandatory microphone. Coordinate #76's canonical signal narrative without overwriting it. #473/#451.

## 7. Counterweight Loft

**Objective:** latch a service platform, recover a small tool and use it at a different height without dropping an essential resource.

**Proving idea:** a left pan has one fixed unit; movable weights are 1,3,4,5. Equal totals require seven per pan: movable 1+5 joins the fixed unit on the left; 3+4 goes right. Once a mechanical latch is deliberately engaged, a reusable piece can move to a later task. A missing latch must produce a reversible state change, not destroy the tool.

**Aha:** distinguish balancing, latching and resource reuse as separate operations. **Acceptance:** validate every placement/removal order and all equivalent left/right arrangements allowed by the actual fixture. A solver's preferred shortest sequence must not become the only accepted playthrough. No real physics simulation is necessary. #473.

## 8. Mapmaker's Fold

**Objective:** reconstruct a service route by aligning two survey sheets and opening a panel at the matching junction.

**Structure:** three stable anchors fix alignment; dated wall changes disambiguate two initially plausible overlays. A fold or rotation transforms how labels relate without changing the underlying map. The player chooses named transformations through both pointer and text controls.

**Transformation:** an apparently decorative margin becomes a route instruction once aligned. **Acceptance:** author exact coordinates and adjacency rules, enumerate all allowed transforms, retain counterexamples before the third anchor and test the route against the actual room state. Do not infer motive from a missing map annotation. This is a separate anthology treatment supporting #53's methods, not a competing Wrenmere chapter. #473.

## 9. Lost Property Counter

**Objective:** release the correct stored parcel by matching persistent physical identity, repair records and claim receipts.

**Structure:** label names were changed, while seals and repairs persist. Two parallel trails establish when a tag moved and which object bears the recorded repair. A shared key opens compartments in either order; returning it is always possible.

**Transformation:** inspecting a parcel's lining reveals a second record that changes the meaning of an earlier receipt. **Aha:** names and containers can change without the object becoming a different object. **Acceptance:** explicitly test alias confusion and equally plausible claimants. Never turn an uncertain identification into a forced accusation. The exact source packet must identify what is known before each action. #474/#446.

## 10. Projection Booth

**Objective:** align a silent demonstration film to reveal a maintenance instruction and reopen the booth shutter.

**Structure:** three strips carry registration markers and partial shapes. The order is constrained by recorded transitions rather than arbitrary aesthetic preference. The player may compare still frames and step through changes; playback timing is never essential.

**Transformation:** a correct overlap creates an inspectable image/description unavailable on any one strip. **Acceptance:** supply complete frame-by-frame text equivalents, allow reversal and reset, and verify that multiple visually similar alignments are distinguished by a real clue. Start with static diagrams, not licensed footage or expensive video generation. The room fails its design review if it becomes an inaccessible pixel hunt. #474/#470.

## 11. Clockmaker's Rehearsal

**Objective:** coordinate three demonstration dials to open a tool chest. Time here is a manipulable discrete model, not a real countdown.

**Proving idea:** over 0-59 simulated turns, dials cycle through 3,4 and5 positions. Their target remainders are 1,2 and4. Thirty-four is the unique matching turn count in that range. All cycles and targets are supplied in-room; a scratch grid is available, and no knowledge of the Chinese remainder theorem is required.

**Advanced extension:** an independently documented reverse gear changes one dial's direction; author and verify a new instance rather than silently changing the first one. **Acceptance:** independently enumerate the allowed turn interval, test wraparound/reverse controls and preserve progress while changing the view. No clock-precision task or waiting for real time. #474.

## 12. House of Departures

**Objective:** reopen a small departure hall by reconciling a transformed plan, a calibrated instrument and a newly printed routing record.

**Structure:** three open leads use familiar methods in different representations. Their results combine into a final route/mechanism, not a concatenation of earlier room passwords. Each necessary method has an in-room recap; ownership of previous packs is not required.

**Transformation:** the central model changes from disconnected stations into a traversable route. The ending shows what was restored rather than merely displaying a completion badge. **Acceptance:** prove each branch and the joint resource model, protect missing/revised anthology dependencies, and test whether the experience feels like a coherent capstone rather than an exam. This is the last production wave, not justification to build three new engines now. #474/#456.

## Shared commissioning gate

Before a treatment becomes a registered room: finish the source inventory, exact dependency graph, clue derivations, intended and alternate answers, state model, hint ladder, story-off copy, reversible recovery, originality/rights check and actual-control acceptance. Record what remains uncertain. Do not call a model-checked but unobserved room human-calibrated or production-ready.
